## 3. Nutrition estimate (per pizzelle = 1/60 batch)

Unrounded estimate: 78 cal, fat 3.7 g (sat 2.0, trans 0), cholesterol 29 mg, sodium ~110 mg, carbs 10 g (fiber 0.2, total sugars 4.4, added 4.2), protein 1.8 g.

*Fat at 3.5 g gives 4% (using the unrounded 3.7 g would give 5%; FDA allows either). Eggs and flour carry some iron and calcium, and baking powder may add calcium, so let the lab decide whether those show 0% or 2%.*

**Serving size:** The FDA reference amount (RACC) for cookies is 30 g. A 20 g pizzelle is about 67% of that. Between 50% and 67% you may declare 1 or 2 units, and at 67% or more it must be 1 unit, so **"1 pizzelle (__ g)" works anywhere in the 15-60 g range.** If a cookie ever weighs under 15 g, the serving must be 2 pizzelle. **Weigh 10 real cookies and use the average.**

**Lab flags:** Confirm fiber and the sugar split, sodium variance (baking powder brand), trans fat (only if margarine is ever substituted), and Ca/Fe/K. Use Eurofins or AGT Labs, ~$300-600 per flavor.

## 4. FDA labeling specs (21 CFR 101.9 and related)

- **Statement of identity** (front, bold): "Pizzelle" or "Italian Wafer Cookies".
- **Net quantity**: bottom 30% of the front panel, parallel to the base, in both US and metric units. Minimum type height: 1/16 in up to 5 sq in, 1/8 in up to 25 sq in, 3/16 in up to 100 sq in, 1/4 in up to 400 sq in.
- **Nutrition Facts type**: "Servings per container" 10 pt. "Serving size" 10 pt bold. "Calories" 16 pt bold, with the number at 22 pt bold. Nutrient lines 8 pt with 4 pt leading. Footnote 6 pt. 0.5 pt box rule. One clean sans-serif typeface (Helvetica Black/Regular). Black on white.
- **Footnote (exact text):** "The % Daily Value tells you how much a nutrient in a serving of food contributes to a daily diet. 2,000 calories a day is used for general nutrition advice."
- **Ingredient list**: descending by weight, common names. Sub-ingredients in parentheses for baking powder and extract. Ingredients under 2% can follow "Contains 2% or less of:".
- **Business name and address** on the information panel (city/state/ZIP is acceptable if listed in a phone directory; for cottage food, Florida requires the operation's name and address).
- **UPC** for retail. GS1 US company prefix, ~$30-50 first year for small business (GS1 US Starter/single-GTIN options).
- **Small packages**: a 6-pack info panel under ~40 sq in may use the tabular or linear Nutrition Facts format if the vertical one doesn't fit.

## 5. Allergen declaration (FALCPA + FASTER Act)

The 9 major allergens are milk, egg, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans, and sesame.

Irma's Pizzelle contains three of them: **wheat** (flour), **egg** (eggs), and **milk** (butter).

> **CONTAINS: WHEAT, EGG, MILK.**

Sesame, soy, peanuts, and tree nuts aren't in the recipe, so no declaration is needed. A shared commercial kitchen may handle nuts or sesame, though. Ask about cross-contact, and add an advisory ("Made in a facility that also processes...") only if there's a real risk.

## 6. Ingredient-line wording and the "organic" question

**Rule (USDA National Organic Program, 7 CFR 205.101(d) and 205.305):** An operation that identifies organic ingredients *only in the ingredient statement or information panel* is exempt from organic certification. It **may not** use the USDA Organic seal or a certifier's logo, **may not** say "organic" on the front panel, and **must keep records for 3 years** proving the ingredients were certified organic (receipts plus product labels/certificates). Your organic eggs, flour, and sugar make up about 79% of the batter by weight. Calling the *product* "organic" or "made with organic..." would require certification. Naming individual ingredients as organic in the ingredient list does not.

**Recommendation (safest, matches "no organic claim"):** launch with plain ingredient names.

> **Ingredients:** Unbleached Wheat Flour, Eggs, Cane Sugar, Butter (Cream), Contains 2% or less of: Baking Powder (Corn Starch, Sodium Bicarbonate, Sodium Aluminum Sulfate, Monocalcium Phosphate), Anise Extract (Alcohol, Water, Oil of Anise).
> *Vanilla: replace the last item with "Vanilla Extract".*

**Permitted alternative (once records are in place):** "Organic Unbleached Wheat Flour, Organic Eggs, Organic Cane Sugar, Butter (Cream), ..." in the ingredient list only.

**Baking powder:** the sub-list above is Clabber Girl's (corn starch, sodium bicarbonate, sodium aluminum sulfate, monocalcium phosphate). Rumford and other aluminum-free brands differ (e.g., monocalcium phosphate, sodium bicarbonate, cornstarch), and some use sodium acid pyrophosphate. **Copy the exact list from the can you use.** Do the same for the extract bottle.

## 9. Distribution path

| | **Florida cottage food (now)** | **Commercial kitchen → Fresh Market (later)** |
|---|---|---|
| Legal basis | F.S. 500.80. No permit needed under $250,000/yr gross sales | FDACS food permit (F.S. 500.12) |
| Where you can sell | Direct to consumers: in person, events, online/mail order to consumers | Retail and wholesale, including The Fresh Market |
| Wholesale | **BANNED.** No selling to stores, cafés, or distributors | Allowed |
| Label | Name/address, product name, ingredients by weight, net weight, allergens; Nutrition Facts only if a nutrition claim is made; **plus "Made in a cottage food operation that is not subject to Florida's food safety regulations." in ≥10-pt contrasting type** | Full FDA label with Nutrition Facts (lab-verified), UPC, no cottage statement |
| Costs | Home kitchen, labels, packaging | Kitchen rent $15-30/hr plus monthly minimums ($120-195), deposits ($200-500), $1M liability insurance, food-manager certification, lab test, GS1 UPC, food-safety audit |
| Fresh Market extras | n/a | Vendor approval packet, third-party lab test, product liability insurance, COOL if imported ingredients (Kerrygold is Irish), 4-8 week QA review. Retail buys at wholesale (~50-60% of shelf price), so $10 shelf ≈ $5-6 to you. |

**Path to Fresh Market:** run the cottage-food pre-order first. Then rent a Tampa shared kitchen, get the food permit and insurance, lab-verify the panel, print the commercial label, get a GS1 UPC, and submit the vendor packet (expect 4-8 weeks of QA).

- **Goal:** 50-100 pre-orders in a 2-4 week window **before** committing to any 10k-unit custom container MOQ.
- **What it answers:** Will people pay the premium? Which flavor wins? 6- vs 12-pack mix? Repeat rate?
- Fulfill under cottage food rules: direct to consumer, cottage label statement, local pickup/delivery or mail.

## 12. What could kill the business case

1. **Labor, not ingredients, is the real cost.** $50k/yr at $10/dozen means **5,000 dozen/yr ≈ 96 dozen/week ≈ 19 batches/week ≈ 29 unpaid hours/week** at 1.5 hr/batch, rising to ~46 hr/week by year 6. None of that labor is in COGS.
2. **Commercial kitchen cost wipes out the margin** at current throughput: $4.50-9.00/dozen at 1.5 hr/batch. The financial model shows about **$30k/yr of kitchen rent in year 1, turning EBT from +$14k to -$16k**. Faster irons or bigger batches are required before the Fresh Market path makes sense.
3. **Wholesale is banned under cottage food.** Fresh Market requires the commercial-kitchen path, which brings its own costs and wholesale pricing.
4. **Breakeven volume:** you need 2,200-4,450 dozen/yr just to cover $20k of overhead.
5. **MOQ commitments:** custom containers at 10k units (~$1,500 at $0.15) before demand is proven. Stick with Packaging Decor cases (144) until the pre-order proves volume.
6. **Year-1 COGS assumption (30%) is below the bottom-up worst case (35% at $10)**. Years 2+ at 45% are conservative.
7. Shelf life and breakage of thin 5" wafers in transit (mail orders) haven't been tested.
